# 34. Synthetic Case Study: Meridian Global Carrier

Meridian Global Carrier is a fictional global airline operating through regional business units. Its strategy requires a unification operating model: shared customer, flight, employee, safety, and operational data combined with standardized core processes.

## Baseline

Meridian has multiple reservation systems, regional customer identities, inconsistent disruption handling, duplicated operational dashboards, and fragmented cloud accounts. Local teams optimize successfully, but the enterprise cannot present one reliable view of the passenger or recover coherently from network-wide disruption.

## Architecture Vision

The target is a shared digital nervous system with:

- canonical customer and journey identities;
- event-driven operational state;
- standardized disruption workflows;
- zero-trust landing zones;
- shared AI and data capabilities;
- regional policy overlays;
- measurable operational resilience.

## Architecture Graph

The graph links business capabilities such as Customer Recovery and Network Operations to information objects, application services, events, environments, policies, and owners.

A disruption event entails:

- passenger-impact calculation;
- rebooking options;
- communication policy;
- compensation eligibility;
- crew and aircraft constraints;
- regional consumer-protection rules;
- operational metrics;
- evidence.

## Transition

MFW produces a partial order:

1. establish canonical identity and event vocabulary;
2. deploy event adapters beside legacy systems;
3. generate consumer conformance tests;
4. activate shared disruption services region by region;
5. observe dual-run correspondence;
6. migrate decision authority;
7. retire legacy identities and workflows.

## Combinatorial Projections

The same journey ontology generates:

- event schemas;
- service APIs;
- SDKs;
- operational dashboards;
- disruption workflow models;
- policy checks;
- customer communications;
- chaos scenarios;
- OCEL mappings;
- migration reports.

## Capacity

The case stress-tests global event volume, ontology composition, rule materialization, regional overlays, and peak disruption scenarios. Warnings are based on workload envelope rather than count of regions or files.

## Governance

Regional variation is admitted only where policy or operating conditions require it. Core customer identity and safety process semantics remain standardized.

## Crown Evidence

The case succeeds when one simulated network disruption can be traced from strategic capability through generated services and policies to observed process conformance, passenger outcome, and replayable receipts.

The case fails if central unification removes lawful regional variation, if local systems retain hidden identities, or if runtime evidence cannot be connected to the architecture claim.
