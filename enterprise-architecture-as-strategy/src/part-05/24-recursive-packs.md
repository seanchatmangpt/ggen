# 24. Recursive Packs and Progressive Binding

Packs can manufacture packs.

This creates vertical Combinatorial Maximalism:

```text
foundation pack
-> domain pack
-> organization overlay
-> environment pack
-> artifact family
```

A generic service pack may manufacture a regulated-service pack after binding data-classification law. The regulated-service pack may manufacture a regional pack after binding jurisdiction. The regional pack may manufacture a deployment pack after binding cloud and environment.

The recursive model supports reuse at the correct semantic level. Public packs remain stable. Organization packs capture private policy. Environment packs capture volatile operational context.

Three laws are required.

## Authority Monotonicity

Embedding a pack must not increase its authority automatically.

$$
Authority(embedded) \le Authority(admitted)
$$

## Export Closure

Every externally observable consequence must be derivable from exported law, declared dependencies, admitted consumer input, and granted capabilities.

## Recursive Receipt

A distribution receipt may summarize constituent receipts, but every consequence must remain traceable through the receipt DAG.

Progressive binding also supports architecture transitions. A baseline pack can generate a transition pack that contains adapters, dual-run policy, data migration, tests, and retirement conditions. After successful migration, a target pack becomes active and the transition pack retires.

The pack graph is therefore an executable Architecture Continuum. Foundation semantics become increasingly specialized solution building blocks without losing lineage.

The risk is uncontrolled recursion. Termination must be bounded by lifecycle depth, dependency closure, or a well-founded obligation measure. Generated complexity is admissible only when unfinished obligation decreases.
